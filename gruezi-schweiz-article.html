The mountains are three hours from everywhere.

Switzerland is small enough to cross before lunch and dense enough to spend a season in. This guide covers the routes, the passes, the hostels and the corner-shop chocolate that make it affordable on a backpack budget.

About Switzerland

Four languages, one timetable

A federal republic of 26 cantons wedged between the Alps and the Jura, sharing borders with Germany, France, Italy, Austria and Liechtenstein. German, French, Italian and Romansh are all national languages, and which one you hear can change between two stops on the same train.

It is expensive — there is no way around that — but it is also extraordinarily easy. Public transport reaches villages of forty people, tap water is drinkable everywhere including most town fountains, and the marked hiking network runs to tens of thousands of kilometres. Plan around the transport first and the rest of the budget follows from it.

The confederation begins

Chapter one

A country built on a promise

Switzerland did not begin as a kingdom. It began as an alliance between valley communities who wanted to be left alone — and then spent six centuries negotiating what that meant. Understanding the timeline explains almost everything a traveller notices: the four languages, the flags on every building, the referendums, and a timetable the whole country arranges itself around.

1291

The Rütli oath

Uri, Schwyz and Unterwalden swear a defensive pact in a meadow above Lake Lucerne. The date is still the national holiday, 1 August.

1499

Effective independence

The Swabian War ends imperial authority over the confederation in practice, a century and a half before anyone writes it down.

1648

Formally recognised

The Peace of Westphalia settles the paperwork: the confederation leaves the Holy Roman Empire for good.

1815

Neutrality on paper

The Congress of Vienna guarantees permanent armed neutrality and draws borders that have barely moved since.

1848

One federal state

After a short civil war the cantons adopt a federal constitution — direct democracy, Bern as the seat of government.

1863

The Red Cross

Founded in Geneva by Henry Dunant. The emblem is the Swiss flag with the colours reversed.

1971

Women vote federally

Late, and contested canton by canton — Appenzell Innerrhoden only followed in 1990, by court order.

2002

Joins the UN

By referendum, and still outside the EU. Schengen followed in 2008; the franc stayed.

Three ways to travel

Pick your altitude

Every trip here is really one of three trips. Choose the one that matches your legs, your budget and how many hours a day you want to be moving.

Rail

The window seat

Base yourself in three towns and let the trains do the climbing. Panoramic routes, lake steamers and post buses on one ticket.

7–10 days · pass-heavy budget

Trail

Hut to hut

Sleep in SAC mountain huts and mid-altitude hostels, walk the marked yellow network, resupply in valley Coops.

5–14 days · cheapest per day

Cities

The short hop

Zürich, Bern, Lucerne and Basel sit within two hours of each other, with Geneva a straight run down the lake. Museums, old towns, river swimming in summer.

4–6 days · hostel dorms
Where to go

Nine places, and why

Tell the map what kind of traveller you are and it dims everything that will not suit you. In print, here they are in full — with the honest version of cost, effort and the best way in.

Zermatt · Valais

Car-free, ringed by 4,000-metre peaks, and the base for the Gornergrat railway. Beds are expensive; the marked trails out of the village cost nothing. Change at Visp for the Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn — cars stop at Täsch, and Täsch and Randa are cheaper bases. July to September for trails, December to April for snow.

Lauterbrunnen · Bern

A flat valley floor between three-hundred-metre cliffs, with car-free Wengen and Mürren on the terraces above. One of the cheapest serious mountain bases in the country: campsites and hostels on the valley floor, and the answer to expensive cable cars is to walk up instead. Twenty minutes by train from Interlaken Ost. May to October; the waterfalls are fullest in early summer.

Interlaken · Bern

The hub between two lakes. Touristy in the centre, but every Oberland train starts here and the lakeside paths cost nothing. One of the densest clusters of hostels in the Alps — book ahead in August. Direct trains from Bern, Lucerne and Zürich; shoulder seasons are noticeably cheaper.

Lucerne

Covered wooden bridge, painted facades, and a lake with steamers that your rail pass may already cover. One hour from Zürich; nearer three from Geneva. Day-trippable from cheaper towns if beds here are full. April to October for the boats; the old town works in any weather.

Zürich

The main gateway. River swimming in summer, a serious gallery scene, and the busiest station in the country a bridge away from the old town. The airport is ten minutes from the centre. Highest prices in this guide — eat from supermarkets, swim for free, and come between June and August for the river baths.

Bern

The federal capital: a sandstone old town inside a river bend, arcades for six kilometres, and locals floating down the Aare on hot days. Central on the rail network, cheaper than Zürich or Geneva, and walkable end to end. Summer for the river; the arcades keep you dry the rest of the year.

Geneva

French-speaking, international, and pressed against the far end of the lake. A good entry point if you are arriving from France — the airport has its own rail station and the TGV runs to Paris. Most hotels and hostels hand out a free public transport ticket. Spring and autumn; the lakefront is best in warm weather.

Engadin · Graubünden

Thin air at 1,800 metres, lakes that stay frozen into April, and the Bernina line climbing past glaciers on ordinary regional tickets. Reached by the Rhaetian Railway from Chur, climbing the whole way. Regional trains cost a fraction of the branded panoramic ones. Late June to October, or February for the light.

Lugano · Ticino

Italian-speaking Switzerland: palm trees on the promenade, grotti serving polenta in the hills, and river gorges warm enough to swim in. Two hours from Zürich through the Gotthard base tunnel. Food is better value here than in most of the country — look for a grotto menu. May to September.

Region in depth

Bernese Oberland

Two lakes, one wall of 4,000-metre peaks and the densest cluster of mountain railways in the country. If you only have a week in Switzerland, most people spend it here — and it is the easiest region to do cheaply. Everything is within ninety minutes of Interlaken.

Recommended landmarks

Ranked by what you get back for the cable-car fare, not by how famous they are. The top two are walkable from the valley if you have the legs.

1

Schynige Platte to First — ridge traverse

One train ride buys a whole day above the treeline, with the Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau in front of you the entire way. The best value in the region by a distance.

6 hours, 700 m of ascent · about CHF 32 up*
2

Oeschinensee — alpine lake

Turquoise water in a rock bowl, reachable on foot from Kandersteg station. Go early; the gondola crowds arrive from ten.

90 minutes up · free on foot
3

Mürren and the Northface Trail

A near-level path along a shelf at 1,900 m staring straight into the Eiger north face. The one walk here that is easy and unforgettable at once.

3 hours, mostly flat · about CHF 12 up*
4

Staubbach and the valley floor

A flat loop past nearly a dozen falls, ending behind the curtain of Trümmelbach inside the rock. Costs nothing and works in bad weather.

2 hours, flat · free
5

Lake Brienz by boat

The greener, quieter lake. Steamers stop at villages with no road in, and the crossing is included on the Swiss Travel Pass.

Sit down · covered by most passes
6

Jungfraujoch — high railway

The highest railway station in Europe at 3,454 m. Spectacular and expensive; on a cloudy day you pay it to stand in fog. Check the summit webcam first — and skip it if the budget is tight or the forecast is poor.

Full day, sitting · CHF 100+ return*

Three days from one bed

Day one — land soft, walk the floor

  • Midday. Train to Lauterbrunnen, drop the bag at the hostel.
  • Afternoon. Flat loop past Staubbach and into Trümmelbach inside the cliff.
  • Evening. Cook at the hostel; the valley shop shuts at half six.

Under CHF 20 in fares*

Day two — the big one, Schynige Platte ridge

  • 07:00. First cog train up from Wilderswil — the light is better and the ridge is empty.
  • Morning. Alpine garden, then the traverse toward Faulhorn with the wall on your right.
  • Late. Drop to First and ride down, or walk out to Grindelwald if the legs hold.

About CHF 32 in fares*

Day three — easy finish, the Mürren shelf

  • Morning. Walk up through the woods to Mürren instead of taking the cable car.
  • Midday. The near-level Northface Trail, straight at the Eiger.
  • Evening. Down to Interlaken Ost and on to the next region.

The one rule

Mountain railways above the main network are only part-covered by any pass — budget separately for those. A pass that looks like it covers Switzerland often stops at the bottom of the cable car.

Passes by region

  • Bernese Oberland. Two lakes and the mountain railway network the regional pass was built for.
  • Valais. Zermatt, Saas-Fee and the high passes. Long legs, so a Half Fare Card earns its keep.
  • Graubünden. The Rhaetian Railway runs on its own network — check what your pass actually covers.
  • Ticino. One long ride south, then short local hops and lake boats to Italy.
Hostels

Beds, split the way your group actually sleeps

The headline dorm rate is not the cheapest stay. Once you count a whole group, the cheapest room mix at one hostel often beats a cheaper bed at another — and an odd number of people can end up paying for a bed nobody sleeps in.

Three ways to sleep

  • Cheapest beds. Everyone in the shared dorm. The cheapest way to sleep here, but bring earplugs and expect a ten o'clock checkout.
  • Share a room. Four-bed rooms are the sweet spot for groups — near dorm prices with your own door. Odd numbers spill into a twin.
  • Privacy. Twin rooms cost roughly three dorm beds. Worth it past a week; rarely worth it for two nights.
The trap

Five people booking twin rooms pay for six beds. The sixth is empty and it is still on the bill — which is why the calculator shows capacity rather than heads.

What to look for

  • Every hostel worth listing is under fifteen minutes from a station. In this country that matters more than the neighbourhood.
  • Ask whether the guest card is included — free local buses change what a base is worth.
  • July and August fill months ahead in the Oberland. Shoulder season is cheaper and quieter in the same beds.
  • Self-catering is the single biggest saving available. A hostel kitchen is worth more than a lower nightly rate.
